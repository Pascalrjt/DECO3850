Version: wall haptics on MotorESP

This copy matches the motoresp-wall-haptics branch. The original page, index.html, still shows the earlier design.

  • Controller: driver 1 and both wall motors are removed. One DRI0044 and the proximity motor remain, and IO19, IO23, IO13 and IO27 are free.
  • RC car: same wiring. Its joystick feedback now goes to the MotorESP by radio.
  • MotorESP: same wiring. All four motors pulse together while the car touches a wall.
  • ButtonESP: same wiring. It moved to ESP-NOW channel 6 with the other boards.
  • Bridge: unchanged.

All five boards: controller, RC car, bridge, ButtonESP and MotorESP. Pins are drawn by label, so match the name printed on your board; the physical position on your dev board will differ.

1. Controller ESP32

MAC 14:33:5C:61:11:40 · sketch Pot_Controller_Haptic_ESPNow · Keyestudio ESP32 PLUS, ElectroCookie solderable breadboard, 1 DFRobot DRI0044 driver, 3 pots, 1 vibration motor

  • The ESP32 connects only to the ElectroCookie board. Every rail and junction dot in the diagram is a soldered row on that board.
  • Power the pots from 3.3 V. The ADC pins read up to 3.3 V only.
  • Driver VCC goes to 3V3. On 5 V the driver needs 3.5 V for a logic high, and the ESP32 outputs 3.3 V. The README still says 5 V.
  • Driver pins are drawn in DFRobot's order: DIR1, PWM1, PWM2, DIR2, GND, VCC on the logic side and M1+, M1−, M2+, M2−, GND, VM on the motor side. Follow the labels printed on your boards.
  • Every ground joins together: ESP32, the driver and the motor supply. A jumper also joins the driver's two GND pins, so the motor current has its own return path.
  • The driver's second channel (PWM2, DIR2, M2+, M2−) is spare.
  • On the Mini PCBs, leave the four corner holes empty so the boards seat in the enclosure cradles.
  • Leave the steering pot centred, and throttle and brake released, while powering on (start-up calibration).

Breadboard layout: two ElectroCookie Mini PCBs

The same circuit, placed hole by hole as in the two-Mini-PCB enclosure model. You are looking down into the open box: ESP32 on the left, pot wall at the bottom, motor wall at the top, supply slot on the right.

  • Each row has two separate 5-hole strips, one on each side of the centre channel. The front half is the side nearer the pot wall (bottom of the drawing), holes a to e.
  • Rows are counted from the ESP32 side: row 1 is nearest the ESP32, row 17 nearest the supply slot.
  • Any free hole in the same strip is electrically the same, so the strip matters more than the exact hole.
  • Tinted strips show which net each strip carries, using the legend colours.
  • The four corner holes of each board stay empty so the boards seat in the cradles.
  • The remaining driver stays where "driver 2" sat in the earlier layout (rows 10 to 15), so a board that is already soldered only needs driver 1 and its wires removed. Rows 2 to 9 of the driver board are now free.
  • Only the proximity motor's slot in the motor wall is used; the other two motor slots stay empty.
  • Assumed: you built the two-Mini-PCB enclosure, and the driver's two pin rows sit seven holes apart (columns c and h). Check the driver against the board before soldering.

2. RC Car ESP32

MAC 68:FE:71:2C:0B:C8 · sketch RC_Car_ESPNow · 2 stepper motors, 1 joystick

  • The joystick is the wall-bump sensor. Every 40 ms the car sends its state to the MotorESP, which runs the wall haptics.
  • The joystick pin labelled +5V goes to 3.3 V, so its outputs stay within the ADC range.
  • Leave the joystick centred while powering on (start-up calibration).
  • GPIO 12 is a boot-strapping pin. If the car fails to boot, unplug the left driver's IN3 wire during power-on.
  • Assumed: ULN2003-style driver boards (IN1 to IN4) with a 5 V motor supply. The README lists the signal pins only, so check your driver's power markings.

3. Bridge ESP32

MAC 68:FE:71:2B:75:D8 · sketch ProximityBridge_ESPNow · USB only

  • The bridge is a Keyestudio ESP32 PLUS with no GPIO wiring. One USB-C cable gives it power and serial data (115200 baud).

4. ButtonESP

MAC C0:CD:D6:81:FF:80 · sketch buttonTrigger · 4 push buttons

  • Each button sits between its GPIO and ground. The sketch turns on the internal pull-ups, so the buttons need no resistors and no 3.3 V wire.
  • The sketch now uses ESP-NOW channel 6. Flash it together with the MotorESP, since the two can only talk once both are on channel 6.
  • GPIO 12 is a boot-strapping pin that must be low at power-on. A button to ground can only pull it low, so button 3 is safe whether pressed or released. Never add an external pull-up resistor to this pin.

5. MotorESP

MAC 68:FE:71:2C:78:BC · sketch spinnything · Keyestudio ESP32 PLUS, one ElectroCookie Mini PCB, 2 DFRobot DRI0044 drivers, 4 vibration motors

  • Wall haptic: while the car's joystick is deflected, all four motors pulse together (100 ms on, 100 ms off, PWM 150). The pulsing stops if the car's packets stop for 500 ms.
  • Buttons: button 1 runs motor 1, and so on. A held button runs its motor steadily and overrides the pulse for that motor.
  • Failsafes: a button motor stops if the ButtonESP's repeated START packets stop for 400 ms. The MotorESP obeys only the ButtonESP and the RC car, matched by MAC address.
  • Driver VCC comes from 3V3, as on the controller.
  • Every ground joins together: ESP32, both drivers and the motor supply.

Breadboard layout: one ElectroCookie Mini PCB

The same circuit, placed hole by hole as in the MotorESP enclosure model. You are looking down into the open box: ESP32 on the left, front wall at the bottom, back wall at the top, supply slot in the far wall on the right.

  • The board is turned so the centre channel runs front to back. Each row has two separate 5-hole strips: the left half (holes a to e) faces the ESP32 and the right half (holes f to j) faces the far wall.
  • Rows are counted from the front wall: row 1 is nearest the front wall, row 17 nearest the back wall.
  • Both drivers straddle the channel with their pins in columns c and h. Logic pins face the ESP32, motor pins face the far wall.
  • Check before powering: the ESP32 ground lands on the drivers' logic-side GND pins and the motor supply − lands on their motor-side GND pins, so the two grounds meet through the driver boards. With a multimeter, confirm that each driver's two GND pins are connected to each other. If they are separate on your boards, solder a wire on the underside from row 6 left half to row 6 right half.
  • Power lands on driver 1's own strips and four jumpers carry it on to driver 2, so the board needs no separate power strips.
  • Motors 1 and 2 leave through the front wall, motors 3 and 4 through the back wall. In each wall, the motor wired nearer the middle of the board takes the slot nearer the far wall, so no cables cross.
  • Any free hole in the same strip is electrically the same. Row 1 is spare, and the four corner holes stay empty so the board seats in the cradles.
